<AnswerBlock>

On Square Share you are the seller, so VAT on your sales is yours to handle. Small sellers usually charge their own country's VAT, if they are registered at all. Once sales to consumers in other EU countries pass €10,000 a year, the buyer's country's VAT applies, and the One Stop Shop lets you declare it in one place.

This page points you to the official rules. It is not tax advice: ask a tax adviser or your tax office about your own situation.

## Who charges VAT on my sales?

You do. Square Share is not a marketplace that sells on your behalf: you are the [merchant of record](/en/reference/glossary#merchant-of-record), with your own [seller details](/en/eu-seller-information) on every product page. When Square Share checkout opens, payments will go into your own Stripe account, so the VAT on them stays yours as well.

## When does the buyer's country's VAT apply?

| Your sales to consumers in other EU countries | Which VAT applies |
| --- | --- |
| €10,000 or less in this and the previous calendar year | Your own country's |
| Over €10,000 | The buyer's country's |

The threshold counts goods sent to other EU countries and electronic services, such as downloads, together. It is the EU's figure, set out on the [One Stop Shop portal](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop_en). Sales to buyers outside the EU are a separate case: Your Europe says that when you sell goods to customers outside the EU, "you do not charge VAT".

## What is the One Stop Shop?

Above the threshold, you would otherwise register for VAT in every country you sell to. The One Stop Shop (OSS) lets you register in your own country only, and declare and pay the VAT for all other EU countries there. You register through your own tax office. See [Your Europe: One Stop Shop](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/finance-and-tax/vat/one-stop-shop/index_en.htm).

## Do I have to register for VAT at all?

That depends on your country's own threshold, below which small businesses can be exempt. Since 1 January 2025, a small business can also use the exemption in other EU countries, if its EU-wide turnover stays under €100,000. See [Your Europe: VAT exemptions](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/taxation/vat/vat-exemptions/index_en.htm) and the [EU SME scheme pages](https://sme-vat-rules.ec.europa.eu/sme-scheme/cross-border-sme-scheme_en).
